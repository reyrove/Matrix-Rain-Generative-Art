00 / Statement Artist Statement

A stream of data, frozen mid-fall, becomes a surface with its own code.

I am an Iranian-born artist working at the intersection of digital aesthetics and generative systems. My work begins with a simple observation: the falling stream — patterned, rhythmic, infinitely variable — has always been a form of computation, long before computers.

Matrix Rain renders a 100 × 100 grid of tiled digital rain. Each composition picks one of six stream styles — classic vertical, diagonal, wave, concentric, binary, or particle — and one of six terminal palettes — classic green, blue, cyan, purple, red alert, or white/gray. Every lit cell draws its own stream column, its own character head, its own brightness gradient.

On top of the rain, a series of effects are optionally layered: scanlines, flicker, horizontal and vertical glitch bands, pixel glitches, a digital overlay of katakana and Latin characters, and a blinking cursor. The result sits between cathode-ray terminal and textile repeat. Each piece is defined by a numeric seed and can be regenerated, documented, and adapted for print, textile, and surface applications.

On AI tools — where I encountered technical obstacles, I turned to AI tools for debugging and code optimization. Every structural, aesthetic, and conceptual decision remained my own.

01 / Streams

Six Styles

Each seed picks one of six stream styles: classic vertical, diagonal, wave, concentric, binary, or particle. Every style uses a different math rule to decide which cells fall — sine waves, radial fields, thresholded binaries, or particle interference patterns.

02 / Palette

Six Terminals

Six terminal palettes are available: classic green, blue, cyan, purple, red alert, and white/gray. Each palette pairs a dark background triple with four bright foreground triples at slightly shifted hue, saturation, and brightness — so every cell picks a different shade of the same family.

03 / Effects

Glitch & Overlay

On top of the rain, a series of optional effects are layered: scanlines, flicker, horizontal and vertical glitch bands, pixel glitches, and a digital overlay of katakana and Latin characters. The glitch is enabled at ~60% probability per seed, and the flicker at ~3% per cell.
